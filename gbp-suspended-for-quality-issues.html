"Suspended — policy violation (quality)": what it means, and the five fixes

This is the most common suspension wording — and the most recoverable. It means Google believes your profile content breaks the Business Profile guidelines. Not fraud. Not a ban. Content.

The five triggers behind most quality suspensions

  • 1. Keyword-stuffed business name. "Bella Vista Dentista | Best Dentist Veneers Implants Vilnius" is the single most common trigger. Your name must match your real-world signage.
  • 2. NAP inconsistency. Your address or phone differs between your website, Facebook, and directories. Google's systems cross-check constantly.
  • 3. Service-area misconfiguration. Hidden-address (SAB) profiles showing address signals, or storefront profiles with no verifiable premises.
  • 4. Review pattern anomalies. Bursts of similar-looking reviews, gated reviews, incentivized asks — the profile gets flagged even if the business is real.
  • 5. Content quality decay. Wrong primary category, near-empty photo set, posts abandoned for a year.

The fix sequence

  • Revert the name to real-world (check signage — it must match).
  • Make NAP character-identical on your website footer, contact page, Facebook, and top directories.
  • Correct the service-area / address configuration to match how you actually operate.
  • Stop any incentivized review activity immediately.
  • Upload fresh real photos; confirm your primary category is exactly your business type.

Then submit one appeal listing what you corrected and offering evidence. That's the whole game — quality suspensions with a clean, corrected profile and a proper first appeal recover at high rates.
