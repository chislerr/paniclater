Google Business Profile suspended — what to do, in order

Your profile just vanished behind a red "Suspended" banner. Phones stopped. Here is the sequence that actually works — and the mistakes that add weeks.

Do not do this first: do not submit a new appeal "just to try," do not edit your business name or address "to fix something," and do not create a new profile for the same business. Every parallel appeal resets your place in the review queue. Duplicate profiles get both removed.

Step 1 — Find out which suspension you have

Open the suspension email (search your inbox for "suspended" from Google Business Profile). The wording matters:

  • "Suspended — policy violation (quality)" — most common. Google thinks your profile content breaks guidelines: keyword-stuffed name, inconsistent details, fake-engagement patterns.
  • "Suspended — red flags / abuse" — Google suspects manipulation: review schemes, bulk edits, misleading info.
  • "Suspended — legal or government issue" — rare; usually needs documents, not appeals.

Step 2 — Audit yourself against the guidelines

Over 80% of quality suspensions trace to the same handful of factors, in this order:

  1. Name violations — anything beyond your real-world name ("Smith Plumbing — Best Cheap 24/7 Emergency") is a top trigger.
  2. NAP inconsistency — your Name, Address, Phone must be character-identical on your website, Facebook, Yelp, and directories.
  3. Address/service-area setup — hidden-address (SAB) profiles need a clean service-area config and no visible address signals.
  4. Review pattern anomalies — bursts of similar reviews, review-gating, incentivized reviews.
  5. Category and content mismatches — wrong primary category, thin photo set, stale posts.

Step 3 — Fix what's fixable before appealing

Google reviewers check whether the violating content was corrected. Revert the name to real-world, align NAP everywhere, remove boundary violations. A profile that still shows the violation gets a template rejection — and a longer wait.

Step 4 — Submit ONE strong appeal

The reinstatement form asks what you've done to comply. Your appeal should include:

  • That you serve customers at your location (or the service-area explanation)
  • Days suspended — this is real, documented urgency
  • The specific corrections you made, listed
  • Offer of evidence: exterior signage photo, business registration, utility bill, video walkthrough

Step 5 — Wait, then escalate the right way

Typical first-review time is days to ~2 weeks. If rejected: fix whatever the rejection email mentions, then respond once with added evidence — do not re-submit identical appeals. Repeated identical submissions are the #1 self-inflicted delay.
